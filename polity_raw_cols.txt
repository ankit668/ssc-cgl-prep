MAKING OF CONSTITUTION

¢ A Constitution lays out certain ideals that form the basis
of the kind of country a nation aspires to be.

¢ Tradition of Constitution began in: USA (in 1787).

e Independence of USA: 4th July 1776.

¢ While all democratic countries are likely to have a
Constitution, it is not necessary that all countries that have
a Constitution are democratic.

¢ Democratic Government: A democratic government is a
form of government in which power is held by the people
and exercised through their elected representatives.

Forms of Government:

*¢ Communist: Source or means of production controlled by
government. E.g. China.

* Oligarchic: A government ruled by a few. E.g. China,
Russia.

 Monarchic: A government by a single person specifically
King or a monarch. E.g. Saudi Arabia, UK.

° Totalitarian: It refers to total control over lives of citizens
by whoever is ruling E.g. North Korea.

State List Subjects: Initially 66, presently 61 subjects.
(Public order, local government, health, pilgrimage, liquor,
agriculture, fisheries, betting and gambling, Co-operative
societies, Land, Lotteries).

Five scenarios in which Parliament/Union can legislate on
State list subjects-

1) National Emergency (Article 250): Law valid 6 months
after emergency ends.

2) President’s Rule (Article 356): Law continues until
repealed/amended later or if LS passes a resolution
through Simple Majority.

3) If two or more state legislature requests (Article 252):
Permanent until repealed by parliament, states cannot
amend it themselves.

4) To enforce international treaty (Article 253).

5) If Rajya Sabha gives Parliament this power (by special
majority) in National Interest (Article 249): A resolution
thus passed is valid for one year + 6 months after expiry.

Concurrent List Subjects: Initially 47, presently 52 subjects.
Both State and Centre can make laws on subjects given in
this list. But if both make law Centre's law will prevail
over States’ law. States’ law can prevail only in 1 case
when the States’ Bill is given assent by President. (Marriage
and divorce, criminal laws, code of criminal procedure,
insolvency and bankruptcy, trade and labour disputes,
electricity, education, weights and measures, etc.)

Parliament can later nullify such a state law by passing

another law on the same subject.

* These subjects are not included in Union/State/Concurrent

* Parliament has the power to legislate on residuary subjects.

¢ Source of power of Constitution

— “WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA

¢ Nature of the Constitution
SOVEREIGN

e People have supreme right to make decisions on internal

SOCIALIST
¢ Wealth is generated socially and should be shared equally
by society.

¢ Government should regulate the ownership of land and
industry to reduce socio-economic inequalities.

Example: Russia

> Capitalistic: USA

> Mixed: India

¢ The term ‘Socialism’ derives its inspiration from the

Types of Socialism:

¢ Radical Socialism: The idea of ending private ownership
of businesses and wealth and making everything shared so
that everyone is equal (Classless Society).

¢ Gandhian Socialism: A system promoting Non-violence,
Self-reliance and fair wealth distribution through village-
centered economy.

¢ Nehruism Socialism: A system emphasizing state-
controlled development to achieve economic growth and
social equality. It is also known as Mixed Socialism.

Is Preamble a Part of Constitution and is it Amendable?

Berubari Union Case: 1960

¢ Supreme Court held that-

i) The Preamble is not a part of the Constitution & hence
cannot be amended. :

ii) It is just a key to understanding the Constitution.

iii) It is not legally enforceable.

e In this case, Supreme Court Overruled the Berubari view

and held that —

i) The Preamble is a part of the Constitution and hence can
be amended under Article 368.

ii) It reflects the basic structure of the Constitution (decided
by the Supreme Court).

LIC of India Case: 1995

i) Reaffirmed the Kesavananda Bharati case ruling.

Historical Background
© Queen Elizabeth I granted the East India Company a
charter (license) on December 31, 1600.

The charter gave the company the exclusive right to trade
with the East including India.

THE COMPANY RULE (1773-1858)
Regulating Act 1773:

1. Governor of Bengal was converted to Governor General
of Bengal.

2. 1st Governor General of Bengal: Warren Hastings

3. Creation of executive council of 4 members to assist
Governor General.

4. Bombay and Madras Presidencies made subordinate to
Bengal.

5. Establishment of Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774),
(1 Chief Justice + 3 other judges).

6. It prohibited the company servants from taking any Bribe
or Presents from Indians. :

7. Court of Directors (to manage commercial functions) -
will report on its Revenue, Civil and Military affairs.

Warren Hastings: The last Governor of Bengal + 1st
Gov. General of Bengal.

Elijah Impey: 1st Chief Justice of SC (Calcutta)
pre-independence.

Justice Harilal Kania: 1st Chief Justice of India
post independence.

Amending Act, 1781:
1. It was passed to rectify the defects of regulating act 1773,
it is also known as the Act of Settlement.

2. Exempted the Governor-General and Council from the
jurisdiction of Supreme Court.
Pitt’s India Act, 1784 (named after William Pitt the Younger)
1. It created Board of Control to manage administrative
affairs i.e. dual control system started from here.
2. It was called as “Half Loaf System” as it sought to
mediate between parliament and the company directors.
3. For the first time company territories in India was called
as british possessions in India.

Charter Act of 1793

1. It extended the trade monopoly of the company in India
for another period of 20 years.

2. Commander-in-chief was not to be a member of the
Gov-General council, unless he was so appointed.

3. Members of the Board of Control and staff were to be
paid out of the Indian Revenue.

Charter Act of 1813

1. It formally ended the East India Company's trade
monopoly over the Indian market, reflecting the shift in
British economic policy towards free trade and continued
the monopoly of the Company over trade in tea and trade
with China.

2. It asserted the Sovereignty of the British crown over the
company’s territories in India.

3. It allowed Christian Missionaries to come to India : To
promote Christianity.

4. Rs. 1 Lakh was allotted to be spent on Indian Education.

5. It authorised the local governments in India to impose
taxes on persons and punish the persons for not doing so.

1st Christian Missionary Organisation, Serampore Mission
was founded by: William Carey (in 1800).
1st Christian Missionary in India: Thomas Apostle.

Charter Act of 1833 (Also known as “Saint Helena Act)

1. Governor-General of Bengal converted to Governor
General of India. (1st: Lord William Bentinck)

2. Deprived the Governor of Bombay and Madras of their
legislative powers.

3. It ended activities of EIC as a commercial body, became
purely administrative body (lost monopoly trade with
China).

4. It provided for the establishment of Law Commission of
India to consolidate and codify Indian laws.

5. It attempted to introduce a system of open competition
for selection of Civil Servants.

6. A law member to be in Govenor’s Executive Council.
(Thomas Babington Macaulay was appointed as its 1st
Law member).

7. It completely abolished all commercial activities of the
East India Company and transformed it into a purely

administrative body responsible for the governance of
British territories in India.

Charter Act of 1853
1. Separated legislative and administrative functions of the
Governor General’s Council.

2. Under it, there was an addition of 6 new members to
Council as Legislative Councillors.

3. It introduced Open Competition for Civil Services.

4. It extended the Company rule in Indian Territory, but
period was not specified.

5. Introduced, Local Representation in the Indian Legislative
Council.
